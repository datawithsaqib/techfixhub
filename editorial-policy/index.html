Editorial Policy

Last updated: 6 Nov 2026

TechFixHub exists to give clear, checked answers to everyday tech questions. This policy explains how we choose topics, write guides, and keep them correct.

How we choose topics

We write about real problems people search for — for example, a slow PC or Wi-Fi that stops working. We do not publish topics just to fill a calendar, and we do not mass-generate repetitive pages.

How we write guides

  • Quick answer first. Every guide starts with the short answer.
  • Steps in order. Quick and safe fixes come before slow or risky ones.
  • Simple English. Short sentences, exact Settings paths, and no jargon without an explanation.
  • Warnings before risky steps. If a step can delete data, remove saved passwords, or change security settings, we warn you first.
  • Sources. Windows steps follow Microsoft support guidance, and each article lists its sources.
  • Honest limits. If a fix may not work for every PC, we say so. We do not promise a guaranteed fix, a ranking, or an outcome we cannot prove.

Checking and review

Each article states the Windows version it was written for and the date it was last reviewed. When software updates change a Settings path or a step, we review the guide and update the “Last reviewed” date. We do not change the date unless the article was materially reviewed.

What we do not do

We do not copy other sites’ articles, we do not publish paid placements as if they were guides, and we do not recommend software we have no reason to trust. If we ever earn money from a link or an ad, we will disclose it — see the Affiliate Disclosure.

Corrections

Mistakes happen. When we find one — or a reader reports one — we fix it promptly and record the change. See the Corrections Policy.
